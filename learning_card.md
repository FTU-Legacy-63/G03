## **INSTRUCTIONS**

### **Who are you?**

You are a member of the **Open Market Operations Committee of the Central Bank** of a developing country.

The banking system is facing new changes: liquidity may become excessive or scarce, interbank rates may change, and the economy is also under pressure from multiple factors.

Your task is to monitor the situation, make appropriate OMO decisions and observe the consequences of those decisions. Each decision can affect not only liquidity, but also interbank rates and economic growth.

### **What do you need to do?**

In each phase:

**1\. Observe.**

Read the economic conditions and consider what the indicators are telling you.

**2\. Make a decision.**

Choose the OMO instrument, auction method, offered volume, pricing method OMO offer rate that best fit the current conditions. At the start of each new three-phase period, enter your expected inflation assumption before making your policy decision.

**3\. Review the auction results.**

See how commercial banks participated, which banks won, and how the awarded volume was allocated.

**4\. Review the consequences.**

How did system liquidity change? How did the interbank rate respond? How did the economic indicators move?

**5\. Move to the next phase.**

The effects of your decisions carry over into subsequent phases. Always review your previous decision and its consequences before making the next one.

**Tip:** Do not focus on a single indicator. Consider the system’s liquidity position, the scale of intervention, and the potential trade-offs for the economy. Click **“?”** if you want to learn more about an indicator or option.

### **Dynamic OMO and Defensive OMO**

OMO can be used for different purposes. Two important types are **Dynamic OMO** and **Defensive OMO**.

#### **Key difference**

|  | Dynamic OMO | Defensive OMO |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Main purpose** | Actively change monetary conditions to support the central bank's policy goals. | Offset unexpected changes in liquidity and keep money-market conditions more stable. |
| **Main focus** | Policy direction | Unexpected liquidity changes |
| **Key question** | “Where does the central bank want liquidity conditions to move?” | “Is there a liquidity shock that should be offset?” |
| **Can it inject liquidity?** | Yes | Yes |
| **Can it absorb liquidity?** | Yes | Yes |

## **LEARNING CARD**

**1\. Central Bank Overnight Lending rate**

**What is it?**

The interest rate charged by the Central Bank when banks borrow overnight liquidity from the Central Bank.

**Why does it matter?**

It provides a reference for the cost of overnight central bank funding and can place an upper boundary on short-term market rates.

**How to read it?**

A higher rate makes overnight central bank funding more expensive, while a lower rate makes it cheaper.


**2\. Required Reserve Deposit Rate**

**What is it?**

The interest rate applied to funds that banks hold as required reserves at the Central Bank.

**Why does it matter?**

It affects the return banks receive on required reserve balances and therefore influences the incentive to hold liquidity at the Central Bank.

**How to read it?**

A higher rate increases the return on required reserve balances, while a lower rate reduces that return.


**3\. Interbank Rate**

**What is it?**

The interest rate applied when banks borrow from and lend to one another in the interbank market.

**How to read**

Rate increases: Funding pressure in the interbank market is increasing.

Rate decreases: Liquidity pressure in the interbank market is easing.

**Why does it matter?**

The interbank rate reflects liquidity conditions in the interbank market and is one of the key outcomes for players to observe after conducting OMO.

**Note:** The interbank rate in the game is a simulated result generated by the Economic Engine, not an accurate forecast of the actual interbank rate.


**4\. OMO Instruments**

| Instrument | Nature of the transaction | Initial impact on liquidity |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Outright Purchase of Central Bank Bills** | The Central Bank purchases bills from banks and holds the bills. | ↑ **Liquidity injection** |
| **Outright Sale of Central Bank Bills** | The Central Bank sells bills to banks. | ↓ **Liquidity absorption** |
| **REPO (Sale with an agreement to repurchase)** | The Central Bank sells bills with an agreement to repurchase them at a specified date. When the transaction matures, the liquidity absorbed is returned to the system. | ↓ **Temporary liquidity absorption** |
| **Reverse REPO (Purchase with an agreement to resell)** | The Central Bank purchases bills with an agreement to resell them at a specified date. When the transaction matures, the liquidity injected is returned to the Central Bank. | ↑ **Temporary liquidity injection** |


**5\. Auction method**

**What is it?** 
The rule banks use to submit bids in an OMO operation.

**Interest-rate auction**

* The central bank fixes the offered volume.

* Banks compete on bid rate and volume.

* Accepted bids follow the ranking rule.

**Volume auction**

* The central bank announces the auction rate.

* Banks submit the volume they want.

* If demand exceeds supply, allocation can be proportional.


**6\. Pricing method**

**Single-price:** all successful bids settle at one winning rate.

**Multi-price:** each successful bid settles at its accepted bid rate.

**Applies only to interest-rate auctions.**


**7\. Offered volume**

The scale of securities or funds offered in the OMO operation.

* Larger offered volume can create a larger liquidity effect.

* The realized effect still depends on awarded volume and maturity cash flows.


**8\. OMO offer rate**

The rate announced for a **volume auction**. It affects how attractive the operation is to banks and therefore their bidding response.

The OMO offer rate is not the same thing as the interbank rate.


**9\. Scenario liquidity demand**

**What is it?**  
The level of liquidity demand in the banking system arising in the current phase.

**How to read**

| Value | Meaning |
| :---- | :---- |
| Positive (+) | The system has a liquidity shortage. |
| Zero | Liquidity demand is broadly balanced. |
| Negative (−) | The system has excess liquidity. |

When it increases: The system needs additional liquidity → liquidity pressure increases.  
When it decreases: The need for additional liquidity falls → liquidity pressure decreases.  
Why does it matter?  
Liquidity demand helps players assess the liquidity position of the system before making an OMO decision.


**10\. Expected Inflation**

**What is it?**

Expected Inflation is the inflation rate that the Central Bank expects to occur in the future.

**Why is it important?**

Expected Inflation helps the Central Bank consider future inflation pressure, not only current inflation.

The input of Expected Inflation is required once every 3 phases in the game. Therefore, the value may remain unchanged during the three phases between two updates.

**Note:** Expected Inflation in the game represents the Central Bank's expectation of future inflation, not the actual inflation rate.


**11\. Inflation**

**What is it?**

The rate at which the overall price level of goods and services in the economy increases.

**Why does it matter?**

Easing liquidity conditions can support economic activity, but if maintained for too long or applied excessively, it can create additional inflationary pressure.


**12\. GDP Growth**

**What is it?** 

The growth rate of economic output after removing the effect of changes in the price level.

**Why does it matter?**

Easing liquidity conditions can support credit growth and economic activity, while tightening liquidity can put downward pressure on economic growth.
